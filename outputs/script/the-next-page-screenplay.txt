THE NEXT PAGE
An original 75-second short film
Setting: A fictional neighborhood bookbindery in contemporary Addis Ababa, Ethiopia
Language: English draft; local-language adaptation should be reviewed by a fluent speaker.

LOGLINE
On the morning her grandfather plans to close his bookbindery, Hana discovers that
the neighborhood still needs it - just not in the way either of them expected.

CHARACTERS
HANA, 23, an observant young woman who has returned to help her grandfather. Practical,
affectionate, and unsure whether the old shop has a future. She wears a contemporary
white cotton habesha kemis with a restrained handwoven tibeb border and a light netela.
BEKELE, 68, Hana's grandfather, a patient bookbinder whose hands know every repair. He
wears a simple white cotton shirt with a handwoven gabi draped over his shoulders.
SAMI, 12, a neighbor who brings a school notebook he wants to keep. He wears contemporary
school clothes with a small, understated handwoven cotton scarf.

SCREENPLAY

1. EXT. NEIGHBORHOOD STREET - DAWN (8 seconds)
The first light reaches a quiet Addis Ababa side street. A small bookbindery opens its
shutters. HANA steps inside with a brass key in her palm.

NARRATION: The morning the little bookshop was meant to close, Hana came to return one
last key.

2. INT. BOOKBINDERY - MORNING (9 seconds)
BEKELE stitches the torn spine of an old book. The shelves are thinning. Hana watches
him work; neither knows where to begin.

NARRATION: Her grandfather was repairing a book nobody had asked him to save.

3. INT. BOOKBINDERY - CONTINUOUS (9 seconds)
Hana folds an empty carton. Outside, the city moves on. Bekele smooths the repaired
cover, then leaves a space on the counter beside it.

NARRATION: She thought the city had forgotten this small room.

4. INT. BOOKBINDERY - LATER (10 seconds)
SAMI enters, holding a battered school notebook close to his chest. He sets it gently
on the counter. Bekele looks to Hana; she reaches for the mending thread.

NARRATION: Then a boy arrived with a notebook and a story he could not bear to lose.

5. INT. BOOKBINDERY - WORKTABLE (10 seconds)
Bekele guides Hana's hands through a simple binding stitch. The thread catches. She
tries again, slower this time. The key rests beside their joined work.

NARRATION: Bekele gave Hana the needle. Start with what is torn, he told her.

6. INT. BOOKBINDERY - AFTERNOON (10 seconds)
Neighbors begin to arrive: one with a worn paperback, another with a family photograph,
another simply offering a hand. Hana clears the carton and makes room at the table.

NARRATION: One by one, neighbors brought pages, photographs, and time.

7. INT. BOOKBINDERY - SUNSET (9 seconds)
The worktable becomes a small reading circle. Sami reads from his repaired notebook.
Bekele listens. Hana watches the room fill with faces she knows.

NARRATION: By sunset, the shop was full - not with customers, but with voices.

8. INT. BOOKBINDERY - EVENING (10 seconds)
Hana places the repaired notebook on a new shelf. She hangs the brass key beside the
door, ready for tomorrow. Bekele turns off the work lamp; warm light remains in the
window as the street settles into evening.

NARRATION: The shop would open again tomorrow. The next page was waiting.

PRODUCTION NOTES
Keep the performances intimate and unhurried. Treat the neighborhood as contemporary
and lived-in, not as a postcard. The wardrobe references a particular Ethiopian
handwoven-cotton style for this fictional Addis family; do not imply one outfit represents
all Ethiopians, and keep neighbors' clothing varied and contemporary. Let the tibeb
borders and woven gabi/netela textures read clearly without turning them into costume.
Avoid readable generated signage, invented historical claims, and identifiable
real-person likenesses. The English draft is not an Amharic translation; review any
localization with a fluent speaker before recording.
