THE NEXT PAGE
Live-action short film production brief for a video-generation agent

PROJECT
Create an original, dialogue-led short film set in a contemporary Addis Ababa
neighborhood bookbindery. Target runtime: 75 seconds of story plus a 4-second end
card. Deliver both 16:9 horizontal and 9:16 vertical edits.

NON-NEGOTIABLE FORMAT
- Make a live-action, photorealistic film with fictional Ethiopian adult performers.
- Show actors visibly speaking to one another with natural facial expression and
  believable lip sync. Use shot/reverse-shot coverage for conversations.
- Do not make an illustration, animation, slideshow, still-image montage, or
  Ken-Burns video.
- Keep the characters visually consistent between scenes. Do not imitate any
  identifiable real person or celebrity.
- Dialogue is intended to be spoken in natural Amharic, with accurate English
  subtitles. The dialogue below is the English meaning guide. Have a fluent Amharic
  speaker review the localization; do not invent phrases or use a caricatured accent.

STORY
On the morning Hana returns the key to her grandfather's bookbindery, a young
neighbor asks them to repair a treasured notebook. The repair brings people back
to the shop, and Hana and her grandfather decide to open it again.

CAST AND WARDROBE
HANA, 23: Ethiopian woman, observant and practical. Contemporary white cotton
habesha kemis with restrained handwoven tibeb borders and a lightweight netela
shawl. The cloth should look naturally woven and comfortable for working.

BEKELE, 68: Ethiopian man, Hana's grandfather and a patient bookbinder. Simple
white cotton shirt with a handwoven gabi draped naturally over his shoulders.

SAMI, 18: Ethiopian student and neighbor. Contemporary everyday clothes with a
small, understated handwoven cotton scarf. He brings a worn notebook.

NEIGHBORS: A small, varied group in contemporary clothing, with individual
handwoven Ethiopian cotton details. Do not dress everyone identically or imply
that one regional style represents every Ethiopian community.

SCREENPLAY

SCENE 1 - EXT. BOOKBINDERY, DAWN - 8 SECONDS
Wide establishing shot of a lived-in Addis Ababa side street. No tourist
landmarks. The shop shutters lift. Hana approaches, holding a brass key.

HANA: I came to return the key.
BEKELE (from inside): Then keep it.

Camera: Move in from the street; cut to Hana's surprised reaction. Let the
shutters and city ambience establish the place.

SCENE 2 - INT. BOOKBINDERY, MORNING - 9 SECONDS
Bekele stitches a worn book at the worktable. Hana sets the key down. Quiet,
natural performances; the conversation is close and personal.

HANA: The shop is closing.
BEKELE: Not this morning.

Camera: Over-the-shoulder coverage, then a close shot of the needle and thread.

SCENE 3 - INT. BOOKBINDERY - 9 SECONDS
Hana folds an empty carton. Bekele smooths the repaired cover and leaves space
beside it on the counter.

HANA: Who will come here now?
BEKELE: Someone who needs a page repaired.

Camera: Hold on Hana's face before she looks back at the open space on the counter.

SCENE 4 - INT. BOOKBINDERY, LATER - 10 SECONDS
Sami enters with a battered school notebook held carefully in both hands.

SAMI: Can you mend this? My mother's handwriting is inside.
BEKELE: Let's see what we can do.

Hana reaches for the thread before Bekele asks. Sami watches, hopeful but unsure.
Use clear, continuous dialogue and visible speaker turns.

SCENE 5 - INT. WORKTABLE - 10 SECONDS
Bekele teaches Hana a simple binding stitch. The thread catches; she tries again.

BEKELE: Start at the torn edge.
HANA: And if it tears again?
BEKELE: We mend it again.

Camera: Begin on their hands, then show both faces sharing a small smile. Keep
the woven tibeb and gabi visible without turning the clothing into costume.

SCENE 6 - INT. BOOKBINDERY, AFTERNOON - 10 SECONDS
Neighbors arrive with a book, a family photograph, and helping hands. Hana clears
the carton and makes room around the table.

NEIGHBOR: Can I bring something tomorrow?
HANA: Bring a book. Bring a friend.

Camera: Natural overlapping greetings, then a moving group shot. Keep the main
dialogue audible above the room tone.

SCENE 7 - INT. BOOKBINDERY, SUNSET - 9 SECONDS
The neighbors form a small reading circle. Sami reads from the repaired notebook.

SAMI: I thought this page was lost.
HANA: Not anymore.

Camera: Let the room listen before cutting to Bekele's proud, restrained reaction.

SCENE 8 - INT./EXT. BOOKBINDERY, EVENING - 10 SECONDS
Hana places the notebook on the shelf and picks up the brass key.

HANA: Should I take it?
BEKELE: No. We open early.

They share a quiet laugh. End on the warm shop window and the key hanging beside
the door.

END CARD - 4 SECONDS

PERFORMANCE, CAMERA, AND SOUND
- Natural, understated acting; warm intergenerational affection without melodrama.
- Keep each speaker's mouth visible during their lines. Avoid narration over
  dialogue. Preserve natural pauses and reactions.
- Use grounded contemporary production design, practical lighting, close
  textures of paper, thread, and handwoven cotton.
- Record clear dialogue, room tone, page turns, thread movement, shutters, and
  restrained neighborhood ambience. Use an original, subtle score; no borrowed
  recordings or copyrighted music.
- Compose key action near the center-safe area so both aspect ratios can be
  reframed without cutting off faces or dialogue performances.
- Do not generate readable signs or labels. Add accurate English subtitles
  during editing.

DELIVERABLES
1. Eight live-action scene clips named shot_001.mp4 through shot_008.mp4, with
   dialogue audio synchronized to the visible actors.
2. Clean dialogue audio stems and an original music WAV.
3. English subtitle file (SRT).
4. Final 16:9 and 9:16 MP4 exports, each about 79 seconds including the end card.
5. A brief note identifying any synthetic actors or generated media.
